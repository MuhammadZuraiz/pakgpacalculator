How to use this GPA calculator

Enter every current-semester course with its grade and credit hours. The calculator multiplies each grade point by the course credit hours, adds the course points, and divides them by the current semester credits.

  1. Select the grading scale used by your university or department.

Formulas

GPA vs CGPA

GPA usually means the average for one semester or term. CGPA means the cumulative average across the GPA-bearing course attempts in your record. That is why an updated CGPA needs the credits counted behind your previous CGPA.

Quality points: grade points x credit hours

GPA: total quality points / semester credit hours

CGPA: total cumulative quality points / total GPA-bearing credits

Example calculation

If you take two 3-credit courses and score A in one and B+ in the other, your semester quality points are 12 + 10.5 = 22.5. Divide 22.5 by 6 credits and the semester GPA is 3.75.

If your previous CGPA was 3.20 over 60 credits, the updated CGPA is ((3.20 x 60) + 22.5) / 66 = 3.25.

Transparent methodology

What the calculator does—and does not decide

The calculation is a weighted average: each grade point is multiplied by its credit hours, then the total quality points are divided by total credits. We transcribe institution-specific point values from public primary sources and record the source and review date on each university page.

The tool cannot determine which repeat, transfer, pass/fail, withdrawn, incomplete, or excluded courses your registrar will count. It also does not replace an official transcript or conversion certificate.

Questions students ask

GPA calculator FAQ

What should I enter as prior GPA credits?

Enter the earlier credits your transcript actually used to calculate the previous CGPA. These may be called attempted or GPA-bearing credits and can include failed attempts, depending on university policy. Do not include the current-semester courses entered in this calculator.

Can I calculate more than 15 courses?

Yes. Start with the default rows and use Add course when needed. This version supports up to 30 course rows, which covers most semester and yearly course loads.

How do I convert GPA to percentage?

GPA to percentage conversion is university-dependent. Some universities or receiving organizations publish their own formula, while others do not convert GPA directly. HEC lists the stopping of general CGPA-to- percentage conversion in its degree-equivalence process, so use only a rule issued for your exact purpose. See the source-backed conversion guide.

Why is my official GPA different from this calculator?

Differences usually come from grade scale, rounding, repeated courses, failed course policies, lab/theory credit splits, or department-specific transcript rules. Match the scale and credit hours carefully before comparing the result.

Does this calculator save my data?

No. The calculation happens in your browser. This site does not require accounts and does not store your course list or result.
